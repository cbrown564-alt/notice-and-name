Angling

Angling involves making subtle adjustments to pelvic position during penetrative activity.

Top
Rotating, raising, or lowering the pelvis during penetration to adjust where internal stimulation occurs.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: diagram, image
recognize

Recognize

definition

Name

Angling is tilting your pelvis—forward, back, or side to side—to change where internal stimulation lands.

media

See It

How pelvic tilt redirects pressure along the anterior wall

mechanism

Understand

In the OMGyes Pleasure Report (Hensel et al., 2021), about nine in ten women reported using angling. Research suggests that naming a movement can help you notice it in the moment and repeat what works—instead of relying on vague “that felt good last time.”

Non-concordance

Non-concordance is the disconnect that can occur between physical arousal signals and how turned on you actually feel.

Top
When physical arousal signs (lubrication, erection) don't match the subjective feeling of being turned on - and vice versa.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

Have you ever felt mentally aroused while your body showed no physical signs—or noticed physical wetness when you weren't actually interested?

definition

Name

Non-concordance is a mismatch between physical arousal signs (like lubrication) and how interested or turned on you actually feel.

media

See It

Separate pathways for genital response and felt desire

mechanism

Understand

Meta-analyses find only modest correlation between genital response and subjective arousal in women—often cited around 10–30% overlap (Chivers et al., 2010; Nagoski, 2015). Genital response can occur without desire; desire can exist without obvious physical signs. Consent and wanting are communicated, not inferred from wetness or erection.

Cites: non-concordance-source-1, non-concordance-source-2

Sexual self-esteem is how you feel about yourself as a sexual being - whether you view your sexuality positively, feel confident in your desirability, and have a healthy relationship with your own sexual self.

Top

How do you feel about yourself as a sexual person? Do you carry confidence into intimate experiences—or do negative beliefs hold you back?

definition

Name

Sexual self-esteem is how positively you see yourself as a sexual person—including confidence, worthiness of pleasure, and comfort with your sexuality.

media

mechanism

Body Appreciation

Body appreciation is the practice of relating to your body based on sensation, function, and experience rather than visual evaluation.

Top
Valuing your body for what it does and feels rather than evaluating its appearance.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

During sex, are you more focused on how your body looks—or how it feels? Do appearance concerns pull you out of sensation?

definition

Name

Body appreciation is valuing your body for sensation and capacity—not mainly for how it looks in the moment.

media

See It

Touch activating sensory response, not visual critique

mechanism

Understand

Body image research links appreciating body function during sex with greater satisfaction and less appearance distraction (Satinsky et al., 2012). Weight alone does not predict sexual function; how you relate to your body in the moment often matters more.

Clitoral Structure

The clitoris is much larger than it appears from the outside.

Top
The full clitoral organ is approximately 9cm in size, with internal legs (crura) and bulbs extending inside the body.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

Did you know that what you can see of the clitoris is just the tip? The full structure extends about 9cm inside your body.

definition

Name

The clitoris is mostly internal: glans, shaft, crura (“legs”), and vestibular bulbs—about 9 cm total, not just the visible tip.

media

See It

Crura and bulbs wrapping the vaginal canal

mechanism

Understand

MRI and dissection work mapped the full clitoral structure (O'Connell et al., 2005). Anterior vaginal sensitivity often reflects internal clitoral and urethral tissue—not a separate “spot.” Most vaginal pleasure is still clitoral stimulation, accessed indirectly.

Nerve Density

The clitoral glans (the visible external portion) contains more than 8,000 nerve endings - a remarkable concentration in a structure much smaller than the penis, which has approximately the same number spread across a larger area.

Top
The clitoral glans contains over 8,000 nerve endings - approximately equal to the entire penis.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

Have you noticed how incredibly sensitive the clitoral area can be—how subtle differences in pressure or touch create dramatically different sensations?

definition

Name

Nerve density describes how packed with nerve endings the clitoral glans is—roughly 8,000 in a small area, comparable to the penis but more concentrated.

media

See It

Dense neural network in the glans

mechanism

Understand

Anatomical studies count thousands of nerve endings in the clitoral glans (O'Connell et al.). That density helps explain why touch quality—pressure, rhythm, indirect vs direct—matters so much, and why intensity can feel “too much” for some people at the wrong moment.

Cites: nerve-density-source-1, nerve-density-source-2

CUV Complex

The clitourethrovaginal (CUV) complex describes how the clitoris, urethra, and front vaginal wall are interconnected rather than separate structures.

Top
The integrated tissue cluster where clitoris, urethra, and anterior vaginal wall interact - explaining "G-spot" sensitivity.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

Have you experienced sensitivity on the front wall of the vagina—an area that feels different from the rest?

definition

Name

The CUV complex (clitourethrovaginal) is the integrated cluster of clitoris, urethra, and anterior vaginal wall—often discussed as the “G-spot” area.

media

See It

Shared tissue and pressure between three structures

mechanism

Understand

Imaging studies show these structures are physically connected, not isolated (Jannini et al., 2014). Front-wall sensitivity is real because pressure there compresses internal clitoral and urethral tissue—there is no separate magic organ to hunt for.

Cites: clitourethrovaginal-source-1, clitourethrovaginal-source-2

Internal Clitoral Stimulation

Internal clitoral stimulation refers to reaching the internal portions of the clitoris through the vaginal wall.

Top
Stimulating internal clitoral tissue through the vaginal wall - what's often called "G-spot" sensation.
Citation audit: approved
Media policy: image
recognize

Recognize

Have you noticed that certain angles or positions create pleasurable sensations on the front wall of the vagina—different from deeper penetration?

definition

Name

Internal clitoral stimulation is reaching clitoral tissue through the vaginal wall—especially the anterior (front) wall toward the belly button.

media

See It

Pressure on the anterior wall engaging internal crura

mechanism

Understand

Research suggests many “vaginal” orgasms involve internal clitoral stimulation (Foldes & Buisson, 2009). Techniques like angling can improve contact with the anterior wall where crura and urethral sponge lie close to the surface.

Cites: internal-stimulation-source-1, internal-stimulation-source-2
