# Content fact-check, for the clinical reviewer

**What this is.** A machine fact-check of every factual and quantitative claim in
`content.ts`, the instruments' captions and `docs/DESIGN.md`, made on 2026-11-13.
It is preparation for the review by a named clinician or researcher, not a
substitute for it. Nothing in the app has been changed on the strength of it yet.

**How far to trust it.** Full texts could not be opened (the session's network
blocked the journal hosts), so each check rests on one of:

- **[A]** the primary paper's abstract, read through search results
- **[S]** a secondary source (press release, a later paper citing it, press coverage)
- **[M]** the checker's memory of the literature, not re-confirmed

No verdict here was re-verified independently. Section 3 lists what a reviewer
with journal access should open first.

Line numbers refer to the tree at commit `1fe4e94`.

## 2. Checked and found accurate

- **the-gap** (content.ts:1593; the-gap.contract:16-17): 52,588 adults; usually-or-always orgasm 95% heterosexual men, 89% gay men, 88% bisexual men, 86% lesbian women, 66% bisexual women, 65% heterosexual women. Frederick, St. John, Garcia & Lloyd, Arch Sex Behav 2018;47:273-288 [A]. The caption arithmetic is correct.
- **the-gap / pairing**: "about 18% of women say intercourse alone is enough": 18.4%, Herbenick et al., J Sex Marital Ther 2018, 1,055 US women aged 18-94, probability sample [S].
- **angling** 87.5%, **pairing** 69.7%: Hensel et al., PLOS ONE 2021, 3,017 US women aged 18-93 [A]. **rocking** 76.4% and **shallowing** 83.8%: the abstract says "approximately 76%" and "about 84%"; the decimals appear in press coverage [A + S].
- **non-concordance** stat and instrument: r = .26 women, .66 men (Chivers et al., Arch Sex Behav 2010;39:5-56) [A]. The "ask, don't infer" framing matches the data.
- **nerve-density** stat and nerve-density.contract:18: "more than 10,000 nerve fibres" (Uloko et al., 2023: 10,281) [A].
- **pulsing** stat: 0.8 s between contractions, Masters & Johnson 1966 [S], with the lengthening caveat in row 9.
- **asking**: the 55% figure and "most often to avoid hurting the partner's feelings" [A]; "across 93 studies" (Mallory, J Fam Psychol 2022;36:358-371; 38,499 people; r = .43 with sexual satisfaction) [A].

- **clitourethrovaginal** (content.ts:350): "Imaging shows the clitoris, urethra and anterior wall are physically linked (Jannini et al., 2014)" matches the review's abstract [A].
- **clitoral-structure**: glans as the visible tip; body, two crura and two bulbs as one organ; glans "a few millimetres across" [S].
- **brakes-accelerators**: the description of the model is right; only the citation and "outweigh" are at issue.
- **spontaneous-desire** (content.ts:802): Basson's model does distinguish spontaneous from responsive desire [M].

## 3. Not verified: what the reviewer should open

1. **Richters 2006, results table**: is intercourse + manual 71% or 73%? Are 50 and 86 exact? What were the denominators?
2. **O'Connell 2005 (J Urol) and O'Connell 1998, full text**: does either state a total length of "about 9 cm", and measured how?
3. **Hensel 2021, Table 2** (open access): exact 76.4% and 83.8%; the denominator; "ever" versus current use.
4. **Herbenick 2018, tables**: exact shares for pressure, direct versus indirect touch, and motions.
5. **Herbenick 2019, item wording**: whether the 55.4% item asked about touch or about sex generally.
6. **Frederick 2018, methods**: question wording and time frame, recruitment through NBCNews.com, the authors' limitations, and the paper's own definition of the "golden trio".
7. **Sand & Fisher 2007**: direction and size of the FSFI difference for women endorsing Basson's model.
8. **Masters & Johnson 1966 / 1970**: original pages for 0.8 s, the number of contractions, glans retraction and the spectator role.
9. **Uloko 2023**: donor characteristics and whether fibres to structures other than the glans are included.
10. **Hilliges 1995, Pauls 2006, Li 2014**: sample sizes and biopsy sites; none measured "the first third" as such.
11. **Vague sources** (edging, spreading, sexual-self-esteem): no study is named, so nothing can be checked.
12. **The digest PDF itself** has errors not to inherit: "8,000 nerve endings" (livestock origin); it credits the clitourethrovaginal complex to O'Connell (it is Jannini's term); "20+ minutes" and 50/71/86 are sourced to Psychology Today; "r = .26 for women versus r = .16 for men" for orgasm is from Mallory, Stanton & Handy 2019, not Mallory 2022; "the gap is not biological" is stated as proven.

## 4. Safety and clinical scope

- **No pain guidance anywhere.** No mention of pain, a doctor or lubricant in any content or instrument. The Touch chapter and "Reaching Inside" give penetration instructions (content.ts:407, 1312, 1368, 1424, 1479) with only "nothing to force" at 407. A clinician will want: pain with touch or penetration is a reason to stop, and persistent pain, bleeding or a sudden change deserves a clinician. DESIGN.md's "Every pattern shown is a normal one" and its deferral of pain to a later chapter make this gap deliberate.
- **No "not medical advice" or scope statement** in the app.
- **Responsive desire** (content.ts:858): "You do not need to feel 'in the mood' before you begin" can be read as encouragement to have sex without wanting to. It needs explicit willingness and stop-any-time framing.
- **Non-concordance** (content.ts:973): "what did trusting the wrong one cost you?" is a hard prompt for someone whose body responded during unwanted sex. Soften it, and say plainly that a genital response during unwanted contact is not consent or desire.
- **Warm-up, lubrication and timing** assume a pre-menopausal, unmedicated body. Menopause, breastfeeding, SSRIs, hormonal contraception and some conditions reduce lubrication and engorgement however much time is given. "Nothing was wrong earlier. It simply was not time yet" could delay help-seeking. Lubricant is never mentioned.
- **The orgasm gap** (content.ts:1591-1592): "not what women's bodies can do" and "a pattern in the encounter, not a flaw in you" leave no room for medical contributors (medication, neurological or pelvic conditions, surgery, genital cutting).
- **Body appreciation** (content.ts:1140): "Weight alone does not predict sexual function" reads as a medical claim from one online sample of 247.
- **Whose bodies.** All anatomy assumes cis women with unaltered genitals; nothing for women after genital cutting, vulvar or pelvic surgery, intersex variations, or trans women. The nerve count comes from five transmasculine donors on testosterone [M] yet is presented as "the clitoris".
- **Samples are WEIRD.** US (Hensel, Herbenick, Frederick), Australia 2001-02 (Richters), North American or European lab volunteers (Chivers). Figures are written as "of women" without "US" or "in one survey".
- **Heteronormative default in Touch.** Penetration frames five of six concepts. "Women with women, 86; women with men, 65" equates lesbian identity with partner gender, and bisexual women (66%) undercut the simple story.
- **Absolute phrases a reviewer may flag**: "that's how I'm wired, not something to fix" (877); "really internal clitoris" (426); "angle usually beats force" (450); "Deeper is not closer"; "Brakes outweigh accelerators"; "Same bodies, different script".
- **Prescriptive numbers**: "Twenty unhurried minutes changes everything for me" (content.ts:488) and the 5/15/25-minute chips set a norm with no evidence behind it (row 1).
- **Edging and pulsing** present experience as physiology (rows 9, 18).
